Endoscopic

TORe — Transoral Outlet Reduction

Who it's for: Patients who regained weight after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass because the pouch-to-bowel outlet stretched.

How it works: Argon plasma coagulation trims the dilated outlet; the OverStitch device places full-thickness sutures to narrow it to 8–12 mm. No incisions. Day-case under sedation. 45–90 minutes.

Evidence: Mean 7% TBW at 6 months, 8% at 12 months in RYGB regain patients (Springer 2026). Five-year durability supported by DDW oral abstract 641.

Cost at IBC: £3,200 all-inclusive.

Endoscopic

GFMA — Gastric Fundic Mucosal Ablation

Who it's for: Patients whose weight regain is hunger-driven — persistent, non-mechanical appetite even with a good sleeve or bypass anatomy.

How it works: Argon plasma ablation of the mucosa lining the top of the stomach — the fundic region that produces the hunger hormone ghrelin. Day-case, no incisions. Often combined with revisional ESG for greater weight loss than either alone.

Evidence: Mechanism validated by Mayo Clinic and DDW 2025 clinical data (True You). Combined GFMA+ESG boosts TBWL beyond ESG-alone 13.6–16.2% (PubMed 41760139).

Cost at IBC: £3,200 all-inclusive.

Endoscopic

R-ESG — Revisional Endoscopic Sleeve

Who it's for: Patients who regained weight after a surgical sleeve because the sleeve has dilated over time, without significant reflux.

How it works: Full-thickness OverStitch sutures re-narrow the dilated sleeve from the inside. Day-case, no incisions, 60–90 minutes under sedation.

Evidence: Published mean 15.7% total body weight loss at 12 months in the pivotal series (GIE Journal). Overview and safety confirmed by review PMC10354344.

Cost at IBC: £2,900 all-inclusive.

When you need surgical revision instead

Endoscopic is first-line for many cases, but not all. Surgical conversion is the right answer when:

  • Severe reflux after sleeve, PPI-dependent, oesophagitis on endoscopy
  • Very large sleeve dilation not correctable endoscopically
  • Weight regain with recurrent type 2 diabetes — SADI-S is more effective metabolically than R-ESG
  • Chronic obstruction or stricture from the primary operation
  • Very high starting BMI where 15–20% TBWL is insufficient
  • Failed gastric band with regain — conversion to sleeve or bypass

What to send us before your video review

The most important part of revision planning is getting your records to Dr Üstün before the video call. It is why revision consultations from IBC are longer than primary ones — the anatomy and the history need to be understood in advance.

  • Operative report (op notes) from your primary surgery. This is the single most important document. It describes exactly what was done, staple line configuration, any drains, any noted variations. UK NHS patients can request this from the treating hospital under GDPR free of charge.
  • Discharge summary from your primary admission. Records length of stay, complications, discharge medications.
  • Any post-operative imaging. Upper GI series, CT abdomen with contrast, or MRI reports. Even old ones are valuable — they show the anatomy at that timepoint.
  • Recent endoscopy report if you've had one. If not, we will arrange one on day 1 in Istanbul.
  • A clear medication and supplement list. Everything, including over-the-counter and supplements.
  • Recent bloods. FBC, U&E, LFTs, iron, ferritin, vitamin B12, folate, vitamin D, calcium, HbA1c.
  • Weight history — nadir date, nadir weight, current weight, and any known triggers for regain.

Revision FAQ

When should I consider revision bariatric surgery?

Consider revision when: (1) you regained >25% of the weight you lost, (2) BMI is back above 35 despite consistent lifestyle effort, (3) you have PPI-refractory reflux after a sleeve, or (4) your band has failed. Investigation of anatomy comes before revision decision.

Can a stretched gastric sleeve be revised without surgery?

Yes in most cases — revisional endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (R-ESG) re-sutures the dilated sleeve from the inside, day-case, no scars. Published 15.7% TBWL at 12 months. If reflux is severe, surgical conversion to bypass is a better fit.

What is TORe?

Transoral Outlet Reduction — endoscopic re-narrowing of the pouch-to-bowel outlet in patients who regained weight after Roux-en-Y bypass. Day-case, no incisions, mean 7–8% TBWL at 6–12 months, 5-year durability data available.

What is GFMA and does it help with weight regain?

Gastric Fundic Mucosal Ablation — endoscopic ablation of the ghrelin-producing mucosa in the fundus, reducing hunger. Mechanism validated by Mayo Clinic; most effective when combined with revisional ESG.

How much does revision surgery cost in Turkey?

At IBC: R-ESG £2,900, TORe £3,200, GFMA £3,200, band removal £3,400, band + 1-stage sleeve £4,200, sleeve-to-bypass from £5,000, sleeve-to-SADI-S from £5,500. All-inclusive. UK equivalents £8,000–£18,000+.

Is revision more risky than the original operation?

Slightly. Surgical revision has leak rates around 2–4% versus 1–2% for primary. Endoscopic revision has near-equivalent safety to primary endoscopic procedures. The single biggest safety factor is surgeon revisional volume — choose one who does 50+ revisions/year. Dr Üstün: 300+.

How long after my first surgery can I have revision?

Minimum 12 months, ideally 18–24. Weight loss continues for up to two years and anatomy is still adapting before then. Exception: failed bands can be removed at any time.

Can a gastric band be removed and converted to a sleeve in one operation?

Yes — this is the modern default. Published complication rate of 5.3% in high-volume centres, comparable to primary sleeve. Two-stage only if intraoperative findings dictate.

Can I have revision if my first surgery was in the UK, Germany or the US?

Yes. What matters is having the operative notes and imaging, not the country. Most of IBC's revision caseload is exactly this: patients whose primary was overseas.

What records do I need from my original surgeon?

Operative report, discharge summary, any post-op imaging or endoscopy reports, medication list and recent bloods. UK NHS patients can request these free under GDPR within 30 days.

Will insurance cover revision surgery?

UK private insurance almost never covers primary or revision bariatric surgery. NHS revision provision is limited and pathway-dependent. Most patients self-fund. Specialist medical-tourism complications insurance (WorldRisk, Global Overseas) is strongly recommended — £150–£350 for 30 days post-op.

How long is recovery from revision?

Endoscopic revision: 3–5 days to desk work, 7–10 days to physical work, 4–5 days in Istanbul. Surgical revision: 10–14 days to desk work, 3–4 weeks to physical work, 6–7 days in Istanbul.
