Short answer:
No. Ileal transposition (IT) is not an internationally accepted standard surgical treatment for non-overweight or non-obese diabetes patients. It is generally considered experimental or investigational and is mainly performed in a few specialized centers or research settings.

⸻

What Ileal Transposition Surgery Is

Ileal transposition involves moving a short segment of the distal ileum to the proximal small intestine (usually near the duodenum or jejunum).

Purpose:
	•	Early exposure of nutrients to the ileum
	•	Increased release of hormones such as GLP-1 and PYY
	•	Improvement of insulin secretion and glucose metabolism

This concept is based on the “hindgut hypothesis” explaining diabetes improvement after metabolic surgery.

⸻

International Guideline Position

Major diabetes and bariatric guidelines

Examples include:
	•	Diabetes Surgery Summit (DSS-II)
	•	American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery (ASMBS)
	•	International Diabetes Federation (IDF)
	•	NICE (UK)

These guidelines recommend metabolic surgery mainly for:

BMI	Recommendation
≥40	Surgery recommended
35–39.9 + diabetes	Surgery recommended
30–34.9 + uncontrolled diabetes	Surgery may be considered

Importantly:
	•	Surgery is not recommended for BMI <30 in current guidelines.  ￼

Also, guidelines emphasize that new metabolic procedures should only be used in research settings until proven safe and effective.  ￼

⸻

Status of Ileal Transposition

Current global status

Most experts classify ileal transposition as:

Investigational metabolic surgery

Reasons:
	1.	Limited long-term data
	2.	Few randomized controlled trials
	3.	Small patient series
	4.	Lack of international consensus

Early reviews even stated that ileal transposition should not be used routinely until safety and efficacy are proven in well-designed studies.  ￼

⸻

Where It Is Performed

In practice, the procedure has been done mainly in:
	•	Turkey
	•	India
	•	Brazil
	•	A few experimental centers in Europe and Latin America

Often under names like:
	•	Diverted sleeve gastrectomy with ileal transposition (DSIT)
	•	Sleeve gastrectomy + ileal interposition
	•	Metabolic surgery for non-obese diabetes

However, these are not standard operations recognized in international bariatric guidelines.

⸻

Why It Has Not Been Widely Adopted

Several concerns limit acceptance:

1. Evidence quality

Most studies are:
	•	Small
	•	Non-randomized
	•	Single-center

2. Complexity

Compared with procedures like:
	•	sleeve gastrectomy
	•	gastric bypass

IT requires multiple intestinal anastomoses and bowel transposition.

3. Complication profile

Potential issues include:
	•	bowel obstruction
	•	internal hernia
	•	malabsorption
	•	long-term nutritional effects

4. New medical therapies

The rise of drugs such as:
	•	GLP-1 receptor agonists
	•	dual incretin therapies

has reduced enthusiasm for surgery in normal-weight diabetics.

⸻

Practical Consensus (2026)

International consensus today:

Statement	Status
Ileal transposition for BMI <30 diabetes	❌ Not guideline-approved
Metabolic surgery for BMI 30–35	⚠️ Conditional
Ileal transposition	⚠️ Investigational


⸻

✅ Conclusion:
Ileal transposition is not an internationally accepted standard surgical option for non-overweight diabetics. It remains experimental metabolic surgery, mainly used in research programs or specialized centers rather than routine clinical practice.
