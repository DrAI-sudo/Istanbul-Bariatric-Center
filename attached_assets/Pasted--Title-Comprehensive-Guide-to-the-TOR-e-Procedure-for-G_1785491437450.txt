Weight regain after a successful Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (RYGB) is a challenging reality for many patients. While bariatric surgery remains the most effective long-term treatment for severe obesity, the anatomical changes made during the initial surgery can alter over time. One of the primary culprits behind weight regain is the dilation (stretching) of the gastrojejunal anastomosis—the connection between the stomach pouch and the small intestine.

Historically, patients facing this issue had to undergo complex, high-risk surgical revisions. Today, advancements in endoscopic technology have introduced a safer, highly effective alternative: the **TOR-e (Transoral Outlet Reduction)** procedure.

## Understanding Weight Regain After Gastric Bypass

Before diving into the TOR-e procedure, it is crucial to understand *why* weight regain happens. Following a gastric bypass, patients experience weight loss due to two main mechanisms: restriction (smaller stomach pouch) and malabsorption (bypassing a portion of the intestine). 

However, over years, the stomach pouch and the stoma (the outlet connecting the pouch to the intestine) can stretch. When the stoma dilates beyond its intended diameter (usually greater than 10-15 mm), food passes through the stomach much faster. This leads to a loss of the restrictive mechanism, allowing patients to eat larger meals and experience decreased satiety, ultimately resulting in weight regain.

Scientific literature indicates that up to **20% to 30% of bypass patients** may experience significant weight regain over a 10-year period due to anatomical changes like pouch and stoma dilation.

---

## What is the TOR-e Procedure?

TOR-e stands for **Transoral Outlet Reduction**. It is a minimally invasive, endoscopic procedure designed to tighten a stretched gastrojejunal anastomosis (stoma) in patients who have previously undergone a Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass. 

Unlike traditional surgical revisions, which require incisions through the abdomen, the TOR-e procedure is performed *transorally* (through the mouth). Using a specialized endoscopic suturing system—most commonly the **Apollo OverStitch™ device**—the bariatric endoscopist places full-thickness sutures around the dilated stoma, cinching it back to a diameter of approximately 6 to 8 mm. 

This effectively restores the restrictive barrier, slowing gastric emptying and helping patients return to the feeling of early fullness they experienced shortly after their initial bypass surgery.

## Indications: Who is a Candidate for TOR-e?

Not every patient who regains weight is a candidate for TOR-e. Because weight regain can be multifactorial (anatomical, hormonal, and behavioral), a thorough evaluation at Istanbul Bariatric Center is required. 

The scientific and clinical indications for the TOR-e procedure include:

1. **Anatomical Indication:** An upper gastrointestinal endoscopy must confirm that the gastrojejunal stoma is dilated, typically measuring **greater than 10 mm** in diameter.
2. **Weight Regain:** Patients who have experienced clinically significant weight regain (typically defined as regaining >15-20% of the maximum weight lost post-bypass).
3. **Pouch Anatomy:** Ideally, the gastric pouch should be of a reasonable size (not massively enlarged). While TOR-e focuses on the outlet, some surgeons combine it with pouch reduction if necessary.
4. **Failed Conservative Management:** The patient must have attempted and failed structured dietary, behavioral, and medical weight-loss interventions.
5. **Psychological Readiness:** The patient must be psychologically evaluated and deemed ready to adhere to post-procedure dietary guidelines and lifestyle changes. TOR-e is a "reset" tool, not a magic cure.

### Contraindications
TOR-e may not be suitable for patients with:
* Large, complex hiatal hernias requiring surgical repair.
* Severe uncontrolled acid reflux (GERD).
* Active inflammatory bowel disease.
* Unrealistic weight-loss expectations or an inability to commit to nutritional follow-ups.

## The TOR-e Method: Step-by-Step

At Istanbul Bariatric Center, the TOR-e procedure is performed in a state-of-the-art endoscopic suite under general anesthesia or deep sedation. The process generally takes 45 to 90 minutes.

### 1. Pre-Procedure Evaluation
Before the procedure, patients undergo an upper GI endoscopy to measure the exact size of the pouch and stoma. A barium swallow X-ray may also be used to assess anatomy and rule out strictures or fistulas (marginal ulcers).

### 2. Endoscopic Suturing
* The patient is sedated.
* A dual-channel endoscope equipped with the **Apollo OverStitch™ suturing system** is gently guided through the mouth, down the esophagus, and into the gastric pouch.
* The physician visualizes the stretched stoma where the stomach meets the small intestine.
* Full-thickness sutures (absorbable sutures, usually 2-0 or 3-0 polydioxanone) are placed around the circumference of the stomal opening. 
* Typically, **2 to 4 sutures** are placed in a purse-string or interrupted fashion. 
* The sutures are tightened, reducing the outlet from a dilated 15-20 mm down to a restrictive diameter of approximately **6-8 mm**.

### 3. Post-Procedure Monitoring
Because TOR-e is performed endoscopically, there are no external incisions. Patients typically recover in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) for a few hours and are discharged the same day or after a 24-hour observation period.

## Scientific Results and Clinical Outcomes

Multiple peer-reviewed studies and clinical trials have investigated the efficacy of TOR-e. Below, we compile the scientific data regarding the outcomes of this procedure:

### 1. Weight Loss Outcomes
According to landmark studies, such as those published in *Gastrointestinal Endoscopy* and *Obesity Surgery*, TOR-e is highly effective in arresting weight regain and promoting additional weight loss.
* **Excess Weight Loss (EWL):** Studies show that patients who undergo successful transoral outlet reduction achieve an average of **30% to 45% EWL** within 12 to 24 months post-procedure.
* **Total Body Weight Loss (TBWL):** Patients can expect an average of **8% to 15% total body weight loss** over the 1-to-2-year follow-up period. 
* **Prevention of Further Gain:** A critical metric of TOR-e is its ability to stop weight regain. Data shows that patients who do not undergo revision continue to gain weight, whereas TOR-e reliably halts and reverses this trajectory.

### 2. Stoma Durability
The primary concern for patients is whether the stoma will stretch again. 
* Research shows that an adequately tightened stoma (reduced to <8mm) maintains its size in approximately **70% to 80% of patients** at the 12-month mark.
* While some degree of natural dilation occurs over 24-36 months, the stoma rarely returns to its pre-procedure stretched state if the patient adheres to dietary guidelines.

### 3. Safety and Complication Profile
Compared to traditional surgical revision (which carries a morbidity rate of up to 15-20% and rare mortality), TOR-e has a remarkable safety profile.
* **Adverse Events:** The overall complication rate is reported to be less than **3-5%**.
* **Common side effects** are mild and temporary, including nausea, throat irritation, and mild abdominal cramping for 24-48 hours.
* **Serious complications** (such as bleeding, perforation, or strictures) are exceedingly rare (less than 1%). 
* **Repeatability:** If the stoma dilates again, the TOR-e procedure can be safely repeated, which is a massive advantage over invasive surgical revisions.

## TOR-e vs. Surgical Revision: Why Choose Endoscopy?

When faced with weight regain after gastric bypass, patients traditionally had two choices: an aggressive surgical revision (converting the bypass to a more malabsorptive procedure like a Duodenal Switch, or revising the pouch surgically). 

Here is why TOR-e is superior as a first-line revisional therapy:
* **No External Incisions:** Completely scarless. Lower risk of hernias, infections, and wound complications.
* **Shorter Hospital Stay:** Outpatient or 24-hour stay, compared to 3-7 days for surgical revision.
* **Quicker Recovery:** Return to normal activities within 2-3 days, compared to 4-6 weeks of recovery after open or laparoscopic surgery.
* **Preservation of Anatomy:** The natural RYGB anatomy is preserved, minimizing the risk of severe malnutrition or dumping syndrome that can accompany aggressive surgical revisions.

## Life After TOR-e: The Istanbul Bariatric Center Protocol

The science is clear: TOR-e is a powerful tool, but it is only as effective as the lifestyle changes that support it. The mechanical restriction will help you feel full faster, but long-term success requires adherence to post-procedure guidelines.

### Post-Procedure Diet
You will transition through a staged diet similar to your initial bypass surgery:
* **Week 1:** Clear and full liquids to allow the sutures to set and reduce swelling.
* **Weeks 2-3:** Pureed and soft foods.
* **Week 4+:** Gradual reintroduction to a normal, bariatric-focused diet (high protein, low carbohydrate, nutrient-dense).
* We highly emphasize **carbonation avoidance** and **chewing thoroughly**, as gas expansion or unchewed food can put pressure on the newly tightened stoma.

### Long-Term Behavioral Support
At Istanbul Bariatric Center, our multidisciplinary team works with you to identify the behavioral factors that led to weight regain. This includes:
* Nutritional counseling to reset eating habits.
* Psychological support to address emotional eating.
* Physical activity integration to maximize fat loss.
