Who is a good candidate for gastric sleeve?
Global bariatric surgery bodies (ASMBS and IFSO) recommend surgical treatment for adults who meet the following criteria:
	•	BMI ≥ 40 kg/m² (Class III obesity) — surgery is indicated regardless of comorbidities
	•	BMI 35–39.9 kg/m² with at least one obesity-related condition: type 2 diabetes, hypertension, obstructive sleep apnoea, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, severe joint disease, gastro-oesophageal reflux
	•	BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes — supported by 2022 IFSO/ASMBS metabolic surgery guidance
	•	Age typically 18–65 (older patients considered on a case-by-case basis)
	•	Documented failure of at least one supervised non-surgical weight-loss attempt
	•	Willingness to commit to lifelong dietary and lifestyle change
	•	No untreated psychiatric contraindication, no active substance misuse
Who is not a candidate?
This is what marketplace listings usually skip — and what Google’s helpful-content updates now reward. Dr Üstün will decline or defer surgery for patients with any of the following:
	•	Untreated severe cardiac disease (unstable angina, uncontrolled arrhythmia, EF < 30%)
	•	Severe respiratory disease unfit for general anaesthesia
	•	Active malignancy or recent (< 12 months) diagnosis without oncologist clearance
	•	Untreated bleeding disorder or uncorrected coagulopathy
	•	Pregnancy or planned pregnancy within 18 months
	•	Severe, untreated psychiatric illness — active psychosis, uncontrolled bipolar disorder
	•	Active alcohol or substance misuse (patients need 6+ months of documented abstinence)
	•	Untreated eating disorder — active bulimia or binge-eating disorder without prior therapy
	•	Severe liver cirrhosis (Child-Pugh B or C)
	•	Crohn’s disease actively involving the stomach or duodenum
	•	Large hiatus hernia or severe reflux — a gastric bypass is usually the safer option
	•	Inability or unwillingness to attend structured aftercare and take lifelong supplements
If you have any of the above, our team will explain safer alternatives (endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, intragastric balloon, GLP-1 agonists, or gastric bypass) during your free video consultation.
How the gastric sleeve procedure works
Sleeve gastrectomy is a permanent restrictive operation. Roughly 75–80% of the stomach is removed along its greater curvature, leaving a narrow banana-shaped tube (the ‘sleeve’). The operation is performed under general anaesthesia through 4–5 small keyhole incisions (each 5–12 mm) and takes 45–75 minutes.
Step 1 — Access
Four to five laparoscopic ports are placed. Carbon dioxide is used to inflate the abdomen, giving the surgeon a clear working space.
Step 2 — Mobilisation
The greater curvature is freed from its blood supply using an advanced vessel-sealing device (LigaSure or Harmonic).
Step 3 — Stapling
A 36-Fr calibration bougie is placed. The stomach is divided using 6–8 firings of an endoscopic linear stapler along the length of the greater curvature.
Step 4 — Testing & closure
A leak test is performed with methylene blue or oral contrast. The removed portion of stomach is extracted, ports are closed, and the patient is transferred to recovery.
Two mechanisms drive weight loss: restriction (the smaller stomach holds only 100–150 ml) and hormonal change (removing the fundus dramatically reduces ghrelin, the ‘hunger hormone’).

What is the difference between gastric sleeve and ESG?
A sleeve is a surgical operation that permanently removes 75–80% of the stomach. Endoscopic Sleeve Gastroplasty (ESG) is a non-surgical, incisionless procedure that stitches the stomach smaller through the mouth. ESG suits lower BMIs and is reversible, but produces less weight loss (15–20% vs 25–30%).
